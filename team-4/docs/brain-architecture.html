Monitoring Brain Architecture

The brain is the part of the system that understands the marketplace. It keeps a graph of metrics and how they depend on each other, and computes any metric for any segment from the simulator's data. For every new hour of data it uses both to notice a change, find where it happened and what caused it, and write a report without being asked. It also takes in new metric definitions while it runs.

  • 36 metrics, 299 typed edges
  • 20 modules, 6,624 lines
  • 410 test functions in the 28 test files that import brain/
  • holdout: 120/120 faults detected, 120/120 localized, 116/120 with the cause, 0 false alarms in 457 watched days, 80/80 innocent events silent (results.md)

The metric graph

Drawn from the graph the code loads, not by hand. Every line downward is an exact identity, so a change at the top can be split into the parts each factor explains.

  • Formula edges carry a role: contribution = marketplace_fees + ad_spend − discounts, and everything below is a product. The six boxes in the lowest row of the tree (views_per_session to order_rate) are the step rates of the funnel.
  • gmv and sessions feed two parents. The graph is a DAG, so the effect of a factor on the outcome is summed over all paths: gmv raises fees and raises discounts, with opposite signs at the top.
  • Possible causes are hypotheses with a sign: more stock-outs should lower the add-to-cart rate. They stay assumed in the graph: an incident tests the links of what moved, and the result goes into that incident's report, not into the graph; a question mark means the direction is unknown.
  • Context links a metric to releases of the systems behind it, to the freshness of its data sources and to the promo calendar. A derived metric inherits the sources of its parts.

One tick of the loop

python -m brain.run ticks for every new hour of data, and the live loop ticks again while it waits for the next one. Steps 1 to 5 are the monitor and end in alerts. Step 6 turns alerts into incident files. Steps 7 to 10 are the investigator: they run on every tick for every open incident that has a live alert. Every number in the figure is read from the code when this page is built.

  • Which test. 11 of the 15 watched rates are counts over counts. Such a rate gets an exact test when its spread from hour to hour is what a binomial gives: the two-sided binomial tail, or the same on the surplus for a ratio that never falls below 1 (payment attempts per checkout). Every other rate, money per order for example, is judged on its residual against its own historical spread, on at least 30 of denominator.
  • When a series is judged. After 72 usable hours of history with at least 200 of pooled denominator. The newest 8 hours are read and judged again on every tick, because late data and an injection can rewrite them. An alert is live while its newest bad hour is among the newest three hours; bad hours no more than two hours apart are one episode.
  • Calendar events and late data. A calendar event that covers an alert in time and country explains it when the graph links the metric to the calendar; among the watched rates these are add_to_cart_rate and discount_rate. The record is written with status explained and no incident is opened. Hours held for late data are written as one pending record, which is removed when the data is in and the hours are judged.
  • Resolving. An incident is not closed because the alerts stop. It is resolved no earlier than three hours after the last bad hour, and only once there is enough data that the faulty rate would show, and it does not.
  • The cost follows every path. Through the formulas the outcome is a sum of signed products of 13 atomic factors, and a rate that sits in several products collects from each. Traffic is taken as given: only rates are judged, so a change in sessions is not part of the cost.
  • Doubt is reported. A different place that explains the counts nearly as well (less than 3 apart in log-likelihood) is named under "uncertain". So is a cost that runs through a rate the data does not show yet.

A new metric

A new metric arrives while the brain runs: a file <name>.yaml in incoming_metrics/ with a name and a formula, a query or both. The live loop looks into the folder on every poll. brain/extension.py takes a file once it reads the same on two looks in a row, puts the definition through 12 checks in the order below and writes the answer to <name>.result.json. No language model is used.

  • The file. At most 64 KB; a larger one is rejected without being parsed. A changed file replaces what it defined before, and a text that is rejected withdraws the metric.
  • The query. It reads the four contract tables only, runs for at most 3 s and returns at most 500,000 rows. A file can set the tolerance of its own comparison only above 0 and up to 1e-4; the default is 1e-6.
  • The formula search. 1,045 candidates over the 19 column metrics, each tried on 256 rows first and then on all of them. Formulas with constants or with more than two metrics are not searched.
  • The windows. The query is compared with its formula on all the data. The split test uses the last 24 hours, the check of the whole graph the last 7 days.
  • Aliases. One exception to "reported and not added": when the existing twin is itself a tentative contribution and the new definition has no caveat, the new one takes its place, and the twin's file is answered as the alias.
  • Errors. After three unexpected errors on the same text, a busy database for example, the file is answered as rejected with the error. The loop goes on in every case.

What is not perfect:

  • Causes not confirmed. In 6 of the 240 detected faults (4 of them on holdout seeds) the report does not name the injected cause as supported. All 6 are stockout_surge faults: sports, seed 4; electronics, seed 10; beauty, seed 112 (holdout); home, seed 115 (holdout); sports, seed 118 (holdout); home, seed 119 (holdout). Each is detected and localized, and its report says that no cause is confirmed yet. Without a release to point at, a moved rate counts as the cause only when the second movement that a cause link of the graph predicts shows in the data.
  • What the runs do not test. Holdout means new random seeds of the same simulator: the same four kinds of fault (ad_budget_exhausted, payment_failure, search_regression, stockout_surge) and the same scorer. No two events overlap in these schedules, and data that never arrives is not in them. The hand-over of a new metric is not part of the runs; it has its own tests (tests/test_extension.py, 50 test functions).

The results per run and per kind of fault and the full list of limits are in docs/results.md. That page and docs/results.json are written by scripts/summarize_evaluation.py; python -m sim.score score reports one run.

Not built

  • A euro threshold for alerts. A change is judged by its size against the rate's own history, not by what it costs.
  • Formulas with constants, or with more than two metrics, in the search for the formula of a new metric.
  • Proposing cause links for a new metric. A definition can declare its own; they stay assumed.
  • A range around the cost. The cost is one number, and it is noisy for a short incident in a small place.
  • Judging volumes such as sessions. Only rates are watched.

What "validated" means

brain/validate.py checks the graph against the data: when the live loop starts and writes the graph export, each time the extension agent adds a metric, and on demand with python -m brain.graph --validate. The report separates two kinds of result, measured here on 28 days of simulator data.

52checks on dataThey can fail: a metric has values in at least 95 % of the hours, values stay within bounds, a contributed SQL agrees with its formula.
28hold by definitionNo data can break them: orders = sessions × (orders / sessions). They show the definitions are consistent and are not presented as evidence.
