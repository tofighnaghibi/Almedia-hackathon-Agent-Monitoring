The plain ranking (`scripts/baseline_ranking.py`) is what an analyst could build in an afternoon, scored by the same scorer on the same runs. It watches the same rates in the same cuts as the brain and judges each hour on its own: every series against the mean and standard deviation of its own rate at the same hour of the day over the previous 21 days, leaving out a series whose history there never varied, and it keeps the one series with the largest absolute z-score at or above the threshold. It does not use the calendar, sums over three hours, the targeted test after each release, the search over all places or the investigation of causes. Its cause, always written as supported, is the latest release whose country and platform do not contradict the place (any country when the place names none), from 12 hours before to 2 hours after the first alert; a rollback is never named; otherwise the metric that moved. Of its 13 unconfirmed causes on the holdout at z 50, 12 are stock-outs in a category, where the rule names whatever release falls in the window; at z 12, 18 of 19. The first version gave a history that never varied a spread of 1e-6 and had been run on the first holdout (seeds 101 to 110, not this page's holdout): it found 94/120 at z 12; it named the right cause for 73, raised 396 false alarms (323 of them from never-varied histories) and stayed silent through 33/80 innocent events. Its floor was removed after a review of those alarms, and the threshold was then picked on the built-on seeds only.

## Limits

- Holdout means new random seeds of the same simulator: the same four fault kinds (three of each per run), the same ranges of severity and duration and the same scoring rules as the runs the brain was built on. It does not test other kinds of fault, other sizes or another scorer.
- The late-data events only move the arrival time of a source (ingestion_log); the values themselves are complete. Data that never arrives is not tested.
- Events never overlap in these schedules, so two faults at the same time are not tested.
- One routine deployment per simulated day (random system, country and platform) is in the data as a possible rival cause. Many faults have none close to them, and one of the same system in the same place within two hours of a fault is rare; when it happens the report can leave both supported (see the list above).
- A cause is counted as correct when a supported hypothesis names a release in the fault's place (any system for payment, a search release in the country for search), the pacing release or ad load (ad budget), or the stockouts. The scorer does not check a payment release's system or any release's version, and does not take a point away for a second supported cause, which is why those reports are listed.
- The new-metric hand-over is not part of these runs; it has its own tests (`tests/test_extension.py`).
- Replays test detection across synthetic hours. Wall-clock mode follows real time and cannot compress multi-hour recovery into seconds.
